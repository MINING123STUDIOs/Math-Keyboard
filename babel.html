How this library works

Borges imagined a library holding every possible book: 25 symbols × 410 pages × 40 lines × 80 glyphs. Ours are larger — 262144 pages (4321) of 40 lines × 80 glyphs — with an alphabet of 95 glyphs: the printable base-ASCII characters. So this library holds 95838,860,800 ≈ 5.1×101,659,034,805 books, shelved seven hundred to a hexagon.

The trick is the catalog number. Every book opens with a unique line, and a book's number is that opening line written as a base-95 integer. So whatever you type, there is exactly one book whose first line is your string — search it, and the shelves give it up. A linebreak is not a glyph of the library: type \n in the search box to end the opening line, voiding the rest of it; otherwise every page is a solid block of text. Everything after the opening is deterministic gibberish seeded by the book and page, so a book is always identical on a second visit. Copy a babel:// location and paste it back to reopen the same shelf.

After Jorge Luis Borges, The Library of Babel, 1941 · 262144 pages × 40 lines × 80 glyphs · 95 glyphs · 700 books per hexagon · catalog numbers in base 95
